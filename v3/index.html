Start with what your child loves

Their interests are where learning begins.

Choose one to see how a session might look.

If your child loves music

The song stops. Your child asks for more. That's a goal, met.

Songs, rhythms, and instruments give natural pauses. Each pause is a chance to ask, wait, or take a turn, and the music itself is the reward.

What it builds

  • Requesting
  • Turn-taking
  • Waiting

If your child loves drawing and painting

Need the blue crayon? Ask for the blue crayon.

Painting, drawing, and making give lots of small moments to ask for help, follow a step, and practice hand skills, while making something your child is proud of.

What it builds

  • Asking for help
  • Following directions
  • Fine motor

If your child loves moving and dancing

Copy the move. Then teach it to us.

Movement games build imitation and body awareness. When your child leads the next move, they practice sharing attention with someone else.

What it builds

  • Imitation
  • Body awareness
  • Joint attention

If your child loves pretend play

The puppet is sad. What should we do?

Puppets and pretend play make feelings easier to talk about. Kids practice naming emotions, back-and-forth conversation, and solving small problems.

What it builds

  • Emotions
  • Conversation
  • Pretend play
